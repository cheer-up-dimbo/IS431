4. System Overview

With the problem space and product needs established in Chapters 2 and 3, this chapter presents a summary of the concept development process (Section 4.1), the resulting physical architecture (Section 4.2), and the user journey through a structured training session (Section 4.3).

4.1 Concept Development

Prior to finalising the architecture, concept alternatives were evaluated for two key subsystems: the arm actuation joint and the lower mechanism base. For each subsystem, candidate concepts were scored against weighted engineering criteria and the concept yielding the highest composite score was selected. The full evaluations, including requirements tables, benefits/limitations analyses, and decision matrices, are documented in Appendix 1: Upper Mechanisms and Appendix 2: Lower Mechanisms.

Upper Assembly

Three joint configurations were evaluated against five weighted criteria. The Multi-Actuator Rig was eliminated due to physical bulk and obstruction of the target strike zones; the Windmill Actuator was rejected for permanently obstructing the liver strike zone in the neutral position. The 2-Degree-of-Freedom (2-DOF) Coaxial Differential Joint achieved the highest composite score of 48 and was selected; its coordinated pitch and yaw axes replicate the trajectory of all primary punches while retracting to a guard position that leaves target zones unobstructed.

Lower Mechanism

Two base architectures were evaluated: an omnidirectional wheel system and a decoupled-axis design comprising a motorised linear rail for forward-backward travel and a slewing bearing for yaw rotation. Omnidirectional wheels were penalised by low stiffness under punching impact and performance degradation on the foam and rubber surfaces prevalent in Singapore gym facilities. The decoupled-axis architecture was selected for its positional stiffness and repeatability under repeated impulse loads, with a ball-screw drive and four-point contact slewing ring bearing confirmed via separate decision matrices for the linear and yaw stages respectively.

4.3 User Journey

Figure 7 presents a swim-lane diagram illustrating how the three subsystems collaborate across the five phases of a structured training session. During the Setup phase, only the User Interface is active: the boxer logs in, completes a proficiency assessment, and selects training mode and parameters. At the Countdown phase, all three subsystems initialise concurrently: Robot Intelligence layer ramps computer vision inference from 6 Hz to 30 Hz and resets lateral and depth tracking baselines, while the user adjusts the robot's height via the phone dashboard or touchscreen GUI.

During Active Training, the three subsystems operate in a continuous closed-loop: Intelligence layer detects and classifies user actions at 30 Hz with IMU confirmation; Mechanism responds with arm actuation and yaw tracking; and Interface presents live feedback, a round timer, combo display, and AI-generated coaching tips. A Rest phase between rounds displays round-level statistics, followed by a Results phase in which the Interface presents post-session analytics and the Intelligence layer generates personalised AI coach analysis using a large language model.
