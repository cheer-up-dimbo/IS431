2. Problem Clarification

This chapter presents the research and analysis undertaken to define the problem space, identify user needs, evaluate existing solutions, and consolidate findings into a validated problem statement and value proposition. Primary and secondary research methods were employed in parallel, with findings synthesised through a Value Proposition Canvas framework.

2.1 Domain Overview and Trends

Boxing is one of the most widely practised combat sports globally, and within the sports technology sector, data-driven training tools have moved into mainstream gyms, raising practitioner expectations for quantifiable feedback beyond what passive equipment can provide (FightCamp, 2017; Hykso, 2018).

The global boxing equipment market was valued at approximately USD 4.1 billion in 2023 and is projected to grow through the decade (Mordor Intelligence, 2023), accelerating demand for interactive, data-driven training tools beyond traditional bag work and pad drills.

Robotic boxing products including the Nexersys, STRYK RXT-1 (STRYK, 2020), and BotBoxer (BotBoxer, 2010) have entered this market, but remain predominantly reactive and do not initiate attacks or adapt strategy in real time, defining the capability gap BoxBunny is designed to address.

2.2 Background

Sparring is central to boxing development, enabling athletes to practise timing, defence, and adaptability under realistic conditions. However, as noted by Stiller et al. (2014), repetitive sub-concussive trauma from frequent and intense sparring training constitutes a significant risk for neurological sequelae, creating demand for safer alternatives that replicate sparring's training benefits without compromising athlete well-being.

To better understand this problem, the project team engaged directly with the boxing community by joining boxing gym memberships and observing standard training flows first-hand. User interviews and surveys were subsequently conducted at six boxing gyms across Singapore, and three leading competitive products were reviewed in depth. These findings were synthesised through two Value Proposition Canvases (Section 2.4) and a competitor analysis (Section 2.5), establishing a validated problem statement and the design requirements addressed in Chapter 3.

2.3 Primary Research: User Interviews and Surveys

2.3.1 Methodology

User interviews were conducted at six boxing gyms in Singapore to obtain a broad range of practitioner perspectives. The research method was guided by the Interaction Design Foundation (IDF) empathise-phase framework, employing unstructured interviews to enable qualitative exploration of user thoughts and experiences (IxDF, 2016). Survey forms were distributed to practitioners not encountered in person, to supplement interview data with additional viewpoints. Respondents were classified into two stakeholder groups as tabulated below.

Stakeholder Group Description
Boxing-Gym Boxers Individuals who train recreationally or competitively in a boxing gym environment.
Training Support Stakeholders Coaches, gym owners, and parents who plan, coordinate, and support boxer development.

Interview questions were tailored to each segment and refined into a concise guiding set. The full question set is documented in Appendix 4: User Interview Questions.

2.3.2 Findings: Boxing-Gym Boxers

Interviews yielded 50 boxer respondents. Coaching gaps emerged as the most pressing concern: 80% of respondents cited limited coaching attention in group classes (Figure 1). This is closely related to the second-most common challenge, where 76% of respondents reported difficulty finding reliable training partners at a comparable ability level. Injury risk was raised by 36% of respondents as a deterrent to frequent live sparring. A further 72% expressed frustration with slow skill progression, particularly when constrained by mixed-ability group settings that force experienced practitioners to repeat fundamental drills. Full data is provided in Appendix 5: User Interview Data.

2.3.3 Findings: Training Support Stakeholders

Twelve training support stakeholders (coaches, gym owners, and parents) were surveyed. Priorities shifted toward teaching methods and scalability. Pad work and mitt drills were ranked as the most important training format (Figure 2), as these methods are essential for delivering individualised feedback. However, current coach-to-boxer ratios prevent the consistent one-on-one engagement these methods require, creating a structural capacity gap in gym instruction.

These findings formed the Customer Profiles for the two Value Proposition Canvases, capturing the distinct jobs, pains, and gains of each stakeholder group.

2.4 Value Proposition Canvas

The findings from primary research were synthesised through a Value Proposition Canvas (VPC) framework, producing two canvases — one for Boxing-Gym Boxers (Figure 3) and one for Training Support Stakeholders (Figure 4). Each canvas maps customer jobs, pains, and gains against the proposed pain relievers and gain creators offered by the product, identifying targeted product opportunities for each stakeholder group.

Boxers demand flexibility, quantifiable progress, and the realism of sparring. Primary frustrations centre on coaching scarcity and inadequate partner matching: 80% reported limited coaching attention, 76% struggle with training partner availability, and 72% feel constrained by slow progress in mixed-ability settings.

Training support stakeholders prioritise safety, structured skill development, and operational scalability. Their primary challenge is delivering individualised attention: pad work and defensive drills rank highest in teaching priority, yet current coach-to-boxer ratios prevent the one-on-one engagement these methods require.

Together, the two canvases established a unified set of product attributes that balance boxer demands for realism and measurable progress against stakeholder requirements for safety, structure, and operational efficiency. These attributes directly informed the secondary research scope, guiding the selection of competitor products evaluated in Section 2.5.

Competitor analysis confirmed that existing solutions each address only a subset of user requirements. Digital coaching and performance data are reasonably well served, but realistic striking engagement, adaptability across skill levels, proactive attack initiation, and space-efficient design remain collectively unmet by any single product. These gaps, combined with the Jobs-to-be-Done identified in the VPCs (Section 2.4), directly informed the five design considerations below, which define what BoxBunny must deliver to close the identified market gap.

Table 3: Design Considerations: Rationale, Competitor Gaps & Proposed Improvements
Design Consideration Rationale from Research Existing Competitor Offerings & User Feedback Proposed Design Improvement for BoxBunny
Performance Analytics 80% of boxers cited limited coaching attention; support stakeholders prioritised personalised, quantifiable feedback. Sensor analytics exist across Nexersys and BotBoxer, but metrics are siloed with no product integrating sensor data and visual tracking. Unified system recording punch power, accuracy, and timing, with sensor-CV synchronisation and shared performance dashboards.
Intelligent Sparring System Injury risk deters 36% of boxers from live sparring; support stakeholders ranked pad work and defensive drills as the highest teaching priority. BotBoxer reviews cite no haptic feedback and a stationary base; a recurring finding is that existing systems cannot replicate a real sparring partner. Active arm actuation, footwork body movement, and haptic feedback simulating real incoming strikes, triggered by computer vision.
Skill Progression Studio 72% of boxers attributed slow progress to mixed-ability class settings that constrain advanced practitioners to repeated fundamental drills. STRYK offers multiple training modes but provides no adaptive progression logic linking them. AI-guided drills blending offensive, defensive, and reactive techniques, scaling in difficulty with individual performance.
Adaptive Fight Intelligence 76% of boxers cited difficulty finding training partners at a comparable ability level. BotBoxer dodges in real time and STRYK provides coaching cues, but neither initiates attacks nor adapts based on accumulated session performance. A counterpunching system that reacts dynamically to user attack patterns, augmented by real-time auditory and visual coaching cues.

STRYK offers height adjustment, but user reviews describe it as too bulky for typical gym use. Height-adjustable, portable platform for gym and home deployment, simplified for broad adoption.

2.6 Problem of Interest

User interviews, competitor analysis, and the five design considerations collectively reveal a persistent gap: no existing training technology consolidates performance analytics, realistic sparring interaction, structured skill progression, adaptive fight intelligence, and a modular platform into a single, research-validated system. Competitor offerings each address a subset of user needs, and none demonstrate design decisions grounded in primary user research. This gap defines the problem that BoxBunny is designed to solve:

"How might we design an intelligent boxing training system that can dynamically and safely spar with users while assisting coaches in guiding, monitoring, and sustaining boxer development both during and beyond training sessions?"

2.7 Value Proposition Statement

Based on insights from interviews, surveys, and the analysis of existing solutions, BoxBunny directly addresses the challenges faced by both boxers and coaching staff. The system provides training variety, personalises interaction to individual user builds and performance levels, and introduces structured feedback pathways that passive training equipment cannot offer. The resulting value proposition is stated as follows:

"The boxing robot delivers a safe and dynamic training experience that adapts to each individual, offering the realism of training with a sparring partner and the guidance of a coach, while supporting users of all levels to train effectively, build confidence, and continuously improve."
Design Direction BoxBunny is conceived as a human-assistive sparring ecosystem — redefining how boxing skills are learnt, practised, and mastered by serving simultaneously as an adaptive training partner for recreational and competitive athletes in gym settings, and as a scalable coaching extension for instructors managing groups of mixed ability. The system is not intended to replace professional sparring, but to function as an intelligent, safe adjunct that extends the quality and availability of independent practice.
