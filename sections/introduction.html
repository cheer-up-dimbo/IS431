1. Introduction

Boxing poses serious injury risks, including chronic traumatic encephalopathy (CTE) from repeated head trauma (G., Magdalena, Lindsay, I., & M., 2019). A study of 7,712 athlete exposures across three Olympic Games found that injury risk shows "no difference by sex, weight category, or tournament round," affecting all athletes indiscriminately (Lystad, Alevras, Rudy, Soligard, & Engebretsen, 2021). These risks are highest during sparring, creating demand for training methods that replicate sparring intensity in a safer environment.

The sports technology market is projected to reach USD 55.9 billion by 2028 (Grand View Research, 2018), driven by AI-assisted coaching and real-time analytics. Falling hardware costs and advances in computer vision have made robotics practical for physical training, as seen in tennis ball machines and batting-practice robots used in professional academies.

BoxBunny addresses this gap: a boxing training robot that delivers dynamic punch stimuli and adapts training difficulty without a human sparring partner. The system targets recreational to competitive boxers in gym settings, serving as a supplementary tool to extend the quality of independent practice.
