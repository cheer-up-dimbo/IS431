6. Discussion and Future Work

6.1 Discussion

User Interface. The GUI met all five functional requirements (GUI-1 through GUI-5), including multi-user data isolation, real-time session display, and hardware-independent development. The phone dashboard was added after the CDE AI Fair showed that gloved touchscreen use was still a weak point in the training flow.

Upper Mechanism. The 2-DOF coaxial differential arms, multi-layer padding with IMU sensing, and the CAN bus motor control architecture were validated through iterative prototyping. Arm strike speed (RM-5) and IMU impact detection rate (RM-6) are pending verification with the fully integrated prototype.

Lower Mechanism. The base passed stability testing (RM-1) with a factor of safety exceeding 1.5, and single-person portability (RM-7) was confirmed. Height adjustment stroke (RM-3) and yaw angular velocity (RM-4) are defined but pending verification. System integration tests (coordinated motion, loaded sparring, power budget) require the completed prototype.

Robot Intelligence. All seven Robot Intelligence requirements were verified. The dual-branch (voxel + pose) Transformer exceeded its 90% accuracy target (RI-3, 96.8% on an unseen person) and met the 150 ms end-to-end latency requirement (RI-2, ~120 ms). CV+IMU sensor fusion (RI-4), all five adaptive sparring styles (RI-6), and the on-device Gemma 4 E2B LLM Coach (RI-7) were validated through both automated tests and the CDE AI Fair real-world deployment. The fair also drove the largest design pivot of the project: the unreliable HSV colour-tracking prototype was replaced from scratch with the Transformer-based action recognition model that now powers the deployed system.

6.2 Current Limitations

Subsystem Limitation Impact
User Interface No formal usability testing with target users GUI was validated functionally and at showcases, but structured user studies with boxers of varying skill levels have not been conducted
Upper Mechanism Fixed head-torso pad separation (30 cm) Anatomical head-torso gap varies 26 to 33 cm across the 150 to 190 cm user range; fixed spacing is a compromise
Pending arm strike speed verification (RM-5) 90-degree strike in 0.25 s or less has not been measured on the integrated prototype
Lower Mechanism No linear forward/backward movement Robot cannot advance or retreat to maintain realistic sparring distance; limits footwork simulation
Robot Intelligence CV model training scope is narrow (8 classes, no retraction) Only 8 actions are labelled and annotations stop at full extension. No slips, no head-vs-body targets, no footwork, and the IMU + pad-constraint filter has to cover the model's retraction-phase wobble
Pose estimator not trained for boxing YOLO Pose is pretrained on COCO (bare hands). Wrist keypoints become unreliable at punch extension, forcing the voxel branch to compensate and capping fused-architecture accuracy
Camera field of view limits full-body analysis The RealSense D435i's mounted field of view captures only the upper body. Footwork, stance width, and weight transfer are invisible to the CV pipeline
CV accuracy depends on user framing Accuracy drops sharply when the user is not height-aligned to the camera (see 5.3.4.1). The height mechanism must be manually re-aligned per user, and sparring against differently-heighted opponents is not possible without re-aligning between rounds

6.3 Recommendations for Future Work

Area Recommendation Rationale
User Interface Conduct structured user testing with boxers Formal usability studies across beginner, intermediate, and advanced boxers would validate training effectiveness and identify UX improvements
Upper Mechanism Variable head-torso pad separation A secondary actuator or adjustable rail would scale the head-torso spacing with user height (26 to 33 cm), improving anatomical accuracy
TPU lattice padding replacement A single-piece TPU honeycomb print could replace the foam, spring, and PLA mount, reducing part count and providing tuneable dampening (Di Caprio et al., 2025)
Lower Mechanism Add linear translation (forward/backward axis) Enables the robot to maintain or close distance, replicating a real opponent's ring movement and creating pressure-based training scenarios
Robot Intelligence Expand the CV model's training scope Add a retraction class so the CV model is correct end-to-end, then extend the action set with slips, head/body targets, and defensive footwork. Richer labels feed every downstream module (sparring AI, analytics, LLM Coach)
Fine-tune a custom pose estimator on boxing footage Annotate keypoints on our own gloved-hand footage and fine-tune YOLO Pose on it. An in-domain pose model lets the pose branch pull its own weight rather than relying on the voxel branch to cover for it
Wider camera field of view A wider-angle depth camera (or adjusted mount angle) captures the full body, unlocking footwork, stance width, and weight-transfer analytics while keeping the single-camera portability of the current design
Remove the height-alignment dependency Two complementary fixes: (1) diversify training footage across camera pitches and mount offsets so the model tolerates framing variation, and (2) estimate user height from CV at session start and drive the screw-jack automatically, removing the manual alignment step entirely (see 5.3.4.1)
Structured user-pilot study Controlled pilot with boxers of mixed skill: labelled ground-truth for fusion precision/recall, timed stimulus sequences for reaction-time false-positive rate, interviews for coaching-tip relevance. Replaces the observational evidence in 5.3.4.5 with hard numbers
System-Level Complete pending RM-3, RM-4, RM-5, RM-6 verification Height stroke, yaw velocity, strike speed, and impact detection tests are defined but require the fully integrated prototype

6.5 Conclusion

Project Outcome. BoxBunny addresses the gap identified in Chapter 2: no existing product consolidates adaptive fight intelligence, structured skill progression, realistic sparring, modular hardware, and performance analytics into a single platform. Of the 19 verification criteria defined across all subsystems, 15 have been verified and passed, with four mechanism-level requirements (RM-3 to RM-6) pending full prototype integration.

Subsystem Contributions. The User Interface delivers four training modes, multi-user accounts, and a phone dashboard across 24 GUI pages and 10 Vue views. The Upper Mechanism provides 2-DOF coaxial differential arms with impact-sensing padding. The Lower Mechanism provides a stable base (FoS exceeding 1.5), yaw rotation, and screw-jack height adjustment. The Robot Intelligence layer runs entirely on a Jetson Orin NX as 10 ROS 2 nodes: a dual-branch (voxel + pose) Transformer classifies 8 punch types at 96.8% on an unseen person and 30 fps, fused with IMU pad impacts for confirmed punches; five adaptive sparring styles drive the robot's behaviour; a local Gemma 4 E2B LLM Coach delivers real-time tips and post-session analysis; and a Vue 3 + FastAPI phone dashboard surfaces all of it without leaving the device.

Key Technical Contributions. The dual-branch voxel + pose Transformer architecture for early punch action prediction; the CV+IMU sensor fusion pipeline that turns ambiguous CV predictions into confirmed events through pad-constraint filtering; the Markov-chain sparring engine with weakness-biased opponent AI; and the fully on-device architecture (CV inference, LLM Coach, dashboard backend) that runs the entire training stack with zero internet dependency.
