3. Concept Development: Product Needs Formation

Each design improvement was subsequently translated into one or more verifiable product needs, forming the system-level requirements that define the minimum acceptable behaviour of BoxBunny. These then form the primary input to the subsystem-level conceptual design in Chapter 5.

Together, the product needs in Table 4 define a platform that none of the evaluated competitors individually satisfies: a single integrated system capable of simultaneously supporting teaching, data tracking, and realistic skill development through safe and intelligent sparring.

3.2 Systems Engineering V-Model

The diagram below presents the V-Model adopted as the guiding design and verification framework for BoxBunny (INCOSE, 2023). The left arm decomposes a subsystem from its concept of operations down to implementation, while the right arm verifies at each corresponding level. Dashed horizontal lines represent traceability: every requirement defined on the left has a pre-defined verification method on the right.

This framework applies at every level of the system. Each of the three subsystems (User Interface, Robot Mechanism, Robot Intelligence) enters the V-Model at Concept of Operations and progresses through its own system requirements, high-level design, detailed design, and implementation. Within Robot Mechanism, the framework is applied at a finer granularity: each sub-subsystem (Base, Rotation, Height Adjustment, Padding, Arm Actuation) derives its own requirements, generates and selects concepts, builds, and verifies independently. The V-Model therefore operates recursively, scaling from the full system down to individual components.
