PID Overhead Analysis

Triangular Velocity Profile

A 90° arm sweep (~1.57 rad joint / ~4.71 rad motor) at constant velocity theoretically requires only 0.19 s at 25 rad/s. However, the Damiao DM-J4310-2EC internal PID controller applies acceleration and deceleration ramps that add approximately 0.4–0.5 s of constant overhead regardless of the set speed:

Set Speed Theoretical (const vel) Measured WU→AP Overhead
10 rad/s0.52 s0.85 s+63%
20 rad/s0.26 s0.68 s+162%
30 rad/s0.17 s0.64 s+276%

The velocity profile is triangular (not trapezoidal): the motor never reaches steady-state velocity before deceleration begins. This is the primary bottleneck for strike speed. Tuning the Damiao PID acceleration parameters or implementing feed-forward control could reduce this overhead.

Power Budget Validation

The 43-test speed test measured peak arm motor power at approximately 33 W, dramatically below the 384 W theoretical maximum (16 A stall × 24 V). Three factors explain the 10× discrepancy: short travel distance preventing the motor from reaching sustained peak velocity; the triangular velocity profile; and the fact that current is proportional to torque, not speed — during free punches against lightweight padding (~200 g per arm), the torque demand never approaches stall values.

Raw Data: Full 43-row dataset with 29 columns (including per-motor power calculations at Vbus = 24 V) is available in ros2_ws/unified_v4/strike_speed_results.csv.
Limitations

Strike speed target was revised from the original 0.25 s: the Damiao PID controller's ramp overhead adds an irreducible 0.4–0.5 s overhead; the revised target of 0.70 s was met (0.64 s best). Feed-forward control could reduce this further. Software PWM ramp-down mitigates OVP trips but a hardware TVS clamp remains recommended for full protection under extreme loads. Key load-bearing gears are polymer FDM prints; long-term fatigue under repeated high-velocity impacts has not been characterised. IMU velocity integration was abandoned due to thermal random-walk drift; the system relies on peak-vector magnitude tracking only.
