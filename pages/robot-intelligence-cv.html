5.3.3 Computer Vision & Action Recognition

At the heart of the Robot Intelligence subsystem is a real-time action recognition model that predicts which of 8 boxing actions (jab, cross, left hook, right hook, left uppercut, right uppercut, block, idle) a user is performing — before the punch completes. The system uses a single Intel RealSense D435i RGB-D camera mounted on the robot facing the user. Key constraints: punches come toward the camera with heavy boxing glove occlusion at extension, only the top half of the body is visible, and inference must run on an NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano at 30fps.

Repository: github.com/Yogee-s/boxing_action_recognition

5.3.3.1 Model Architecture

FusionVoxelPoseTransformerModel (~1.75 million parameters):

Voxel Branch (3,456 dimensions per frame)

A 12×12×12 person-centered voxel occupancy grid in gravity-aligned 3D space (optimised from an initial 24³ grid — each voxel covers approximately 13cm, providing sufficient spatial resolution to distinguish punch trajectories while enabling real-time inference). Two temporal delta channels capture motion at different time scales: delta@2 (2 frames at 30fps = 67ms) for short-term punch onset detection, and delta@8 (8 frames = 267ms) for capturing the full punch arc trajectory. A Conv3D stem with 3 convolutional layers (stride 2, BatchNorm, GELU) compresses the 12³ input to a 192-dimensional embedding per frame.

Pose Branch (42 dimensions per frame)

7 upper-body keypoints (nose, left/right shoulder, left/right elbow, left/right wrist) extracted by YOLO Pose estimation. Features include normalised joint coordinates (14 dims), per-joint confidence scores (7 dims), arm extension ratios (2 dims), shoulder rotation (1 dim), elbow angles (2 dims), joint velocities (14 dims), and arm extension rates (2 dims). A 2-layer MLP encodes these to 64 dimensions. The same YOLO model (yolo26n-pose.pt) is used for both feature extraction during training and live inference, preventing distribution mismatch.

Confidence Gating

The pose output is scaled by mean YOLO joint confidence. When YOLO is uncertain (e.g., heavy glove occlusion at punch extension), the pose signal is suppressed and the model falls back to voxel-only classification. This graceful degradation is reinforced during training through clip-level pose dropout (15% probability — entire clips get zero pose) and per-joint dropout (10% — simulates individual wrist occlusion by gloves).

Fusion & Classification

The voxel (192-dim) and pose (64-dim) embeddings are concatenated to 256 dimensions, projected to 192 dimensions with LayerNorm. A 4-layer Transformer encoder with 8 attention heads (d_model=192, FFN=576, pre-norm, GELU) processes a 12-frame rolling window (0.4 seconds at 30fps) with causal masking for streaming inference. Mean and max pooling over valid timesteps produces a 384-dimensional representation, classified by an MLP into 8 action classes.

5.3.3.2 Feature Exploration — Development Journey

The final model architecture emerged through 9 major iterations, each building on the specific failure modes of the previous approach. This iterative process revealed that the key to robust punch classification was not a single breakthrough architecture, but rather the right combination of complementary feature representations.

# Approach Accuracy Key Outcome
1 2D Pose (YouTube + LSTM) High (on YouTube) Validated end-to-end pipeline. YouTube filming angles did not match robot-mounted camera.
2 3D Pose (RealSense depth lifting) ~67% Boxing gloves occlude wrist keypoints at the exact moment detection matters most.
3 Raw RGBD + 3D CNNs (Swin3D, 88M params) N/A Model too large for edge hardware. Batch size limited to 2 on Google Colab.
4 RGBD + Segmentation + Causal Transformer N/A Still pixel-based, still required Colab for training.
5 Depth Feature Engineering (Z-Mask grid) N/A 2D depth grid lost critical 3D spatial information. Hooks vs uppercuts indistinguishable.
6 Voxel + Colour Tracking ~68% Required differently coloured gloves (red/green). HSV tracking unreliable in variable lighting.
7 Voxel-Only Transformer (12³) 80.6% Left/right confusion: 19/56 jabs misclassified as cross (34% error). Identical voxel patterns.
8 Voxel + Pose Fusion 97.3% Breakthrough. Pose eliminated left/right confusion completely. Full circle — pose returned.
9 Production (v11, Jetson) 96.8% Block annotation fix, TensorRT optimisation, parallel processing threads.
Table: Development Iterations — each approach built on specific failure modes of the previous

The project came full circle — beginning with pose estimation on YouTube data, eventually returning to pose as a lightweight complement to 3D voxel motion features. The original intuition about pose was correct, but it needed the right complementary representation (volumetric 3D motion from depth) to succeed.

5.3.3.3 Data Collection and Training

Recording Infrastructure

A custom recording tool was built using the Intel RealSense D435i capturing at 30fps (960×540 RGB + 848×480 depth), mounted at approximately 1.5m height, stationary, 1-2m from the user.

Critical engineering challenge: Early recordings were unknowingly captured at approximately 15fps instead of the expected 30fps. Depth frames were silently dropped because the laptop could not write NumPy float32 depth arrays to an external SSD fast enough. This went undetected initially and only became apparent when model performance plateaued unexpectedly. The solution required a complete pipeline overhaul: a dedicated capture thread running at full 30fps independently of disk I/O, an asynchronous writer thread with a 300-frame queue buffer (approximately 5 seconds of data), non-blocking enqueue to prevent the capture thread from ever stalling, and frame-drop diagnostics tracking sensor-side gaps, timing spikes, and queue overflows. All early data had to be re-collected and re-annotated from scratch — an extremely time-consuming process that underscored the importance of validation at every stage.

Custom Annotation GUI

A purpose-built timeline-based annotation tool (annotate_punches_gui.py) with drag-to-resize segment editing and auto-save every 30 seconds, created to speed up the otherwise very time-consuming manual annotation process.

Annotation strategy: Punch segments start at the first preparatory cue (shoulder drop, hip rotation) and end at full arm extension. Retraction is explicitly excluded because the retraction motion looks visually similar across different punch types and would confuse the model — the discriminative information is in the preparation and extension phases.

Block annotation fix (critical lesson): Initially, block annotations only covered the onset phase (arms rising to guard position). This made the onset of a block indistinguishable from the onset of an uppercut, causing significant confusion during live inference. The fix extended block annotations to include the full sequence: arms rising, holding the guard, and returning to neutral. This single annotation change dramatically improved live block detection — v10 achieved 97.3% validation accuracy but poor live block inference, while v11 achieved 96.8% validation but substantially better live performance specifically because of this fix.

Different camera angles required re-recording and re-annotating the entire dataset, as models trained on one viewpoint did not generalise to others.

Dataset

808 training segments from 2 people (7 clips) + 128 validation segments from 1 unseen person (1 clip), across 8 action classes. Class imbalance: block is the smallest class with fewest samples. Mitigated with class-balanced sampling per epoch and focal loss (γ=1.5) to focus on hard examples.

Key insight — data quality over model architecture: Removing one person's data with inconsistent punching form immediately improved accuracy from 92.8% to 97.3% — a +4.5% gain larger than any architecture change across 6 different model architectures tested. This was the single biggest accuracy improvement in the entire project.

Key augmentation — Horizontal flip (50% probability): a domain-specific augmentation that exploits boxing's inherent left/right symmetry. It flips the voxel X-axis, swaps left/right pose features, and swaps labels (jab↔cross, left_hook↔right_hook, left_uppercut↔right_uppercut), effectively doubling the dataset through a valid transformation.

Other augmentations: speed augment (0.8-1.2×), voxel shift/cutout/stretch, multi-window (9, 12 frames).

5.3.3.4 Ablation Study

A branch ablation study demonstrates that the two feature streams provide genuinely complementary information — neither alone achieves the performance of the fused model:

Mode Validation Accuracy What It Captures
Pose only 44.1% Joint positions but no spatial/3D motion context
Voxel only 80.6% 3D motion trajectories but cannot distinguish left from right
Both fused 97.3% Complete picture — spatial motion + joint-level discrimination

The specific failure mode solved: Without pose, 19 out of 56 jabs were misclassified as cross (a 34% error rate on jabs alone). This occurred because jab and cross produce identical "straight forward" voxel motion patterns — only the wrist positions in the pose stream reveal which hand is punching. With pose fusion, left/right confusion was eliminated completely.

The voxel branch captures volumetric 3D body motion that distinguishes hooks from uppercuts (horizontal vs vertical arc in 3D space) — information that 2D pose alone cannot represent.

The +16.7% improvement from voxel-only to fused, and the dramatic gap between pose-only (44.1%) and fused (97.3%), confirm that both branches contribute unique, non-redundant information.
