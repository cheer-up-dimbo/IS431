Portability & Deployment

BoxBunny must be moved between storage areas, fabrication workshops, and public demonstration venues. This page documents the transport strategy driven by requirement RM-7 (Portability): the base must allow transport by 1–2 persons between venues without compromising the planted operational stance.

Transport Requirement

The robot's total assembled mass is estimated at 20–25 kg, concentrated in steel structural members, motors, and the height-adjustment column. This mass is significant enough that lifting and carrying the entire system by hand is impractical for routine transport, particularly through doorways, corridors, and across gym floors.

The transport solution must satisfy several constraints:

  • Operable by 1–2 persons without tools or equipment
  • Does not require disassembly of the robot
  • Transport features must not interfere with the operational stance
  • Must work on typical gym and corridor surfaces (rubber mats, tiles, concrete)

Gym-Bench Inspiration

The transport strategy was inspired by commercial gym benches, which face a similar design challenge: they must be heavy and stable during use, yet easily moved by one person when the gym layout needs reconfiguration. Gym benches typically achieve this through:

  • Rear-mounted small-diameter wheels that remain off the ground during use
  • A front handle or frame member that allows the operator to tilt the bench backward onto the wheels
  • Tip-and-roll transport — the operator lifts the front, tilts the bench onto its rear wheels, and rolls it to the new location

This approach works well because the wheels do not touch the floor during normal use, so they cannot roll or introduce compliance when the bench (or robot) is under load. The same logic was adopted for BoxBunny.

Transport Hardware

The base features two rear-mounted wheels and a tilt-handling strategy:

Component Specification Function
Transport wheels RMNA100, 100 mm diameter Smooth rolling on gym and corridor surfaces when tilted
Wheel mounting pins KCLSBF12-62 Secure axle retention; standard clevis-pin format for easy replacement

The wheels are positioned at the widest rear edge of the trapezoidal base, complementing the wider rear footprint. During normal operation, the base rests flat on the floor with the wheels elevated slightly or positioned at the same level as the base feet. When the operator lifts the front of the base, the robot tilts onto the rear wheels and can be rolled to a new location.

Planted vs Transport Stance

A key design principle is that transport features must not compromise the planted operational stance. During training:

  • The base rests flat on the floor with its full trapezoidal footprint in contact with the surface, maximising friction and stability.
  • The transport wheels are passive — they do not contribute to the support polygon or introduce rolling compliance.
  • No locking mechanism is required because the wheel mounting position ensures they remain off-ground when the base is planted.

This dual-mode behaviour — fully planted during use, easily rollable during transport — eliminates the need for brake locks, deployment levers, or other mechanisms that could add complexity or failure points.
