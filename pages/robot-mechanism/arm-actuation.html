5.2.5 Arm Actuation

The arm actuation subsystem is the primary strike-delivery mechanism of BoxBunny. Its function is to replicate the upper-body actions of a human training partner — executing targeted punches at anatomically correct striking zones and providing real-time impact detection for training analytics.

Requirements & Considerations

The arm actuation subsystem responds to system-level requirement RM-5:

  • Strike velocity: Execute 90° in ≤ 0.25 s for human-competitive punch speeds
  • Strike types: Jab (pitch), Hook (roll), Uppercut (coordinated pitch+roll)
  • Mass centralisation: Minimise moment of inertia to achieve target velocities within motor torque limits
  • Safety: Multi-layered protection (hardware current limit, firmware dI/dt monitoring, application-level auto-retract)

Required Striking Motions

Design

2-DOF Coaxial Differential Joint

The selected solution is a 2-Degrees-of-Freedom mechanism employing a coaxial differential gear arrangement with two Damiao DM-J4310-2EC brushless motors per arm. The pitch axis delivers vertical rotation (jab), while the roll axis provides horizontal rotation (hook, uppercut). Coordinated combination enables all required strike patterns from a single compact joint.

Mass Centralisation

Both motors are mounted side-by-side at the base of the arm assembly, each driving a pinion gear into the central coaxial gear stack. This concentrates the heaviest components at the pivot point, drastically reducing the arm's moment of inertia — the primary reason the system achieves human-competitive punch velocities without exceeding stress limits of 3D-printed drivetrain components.

Differential Coupling Challenge

The coaxial arrangement introduces kinematic coupling: any roll rotation causes the internal bevel gear to "walk," producing parasitic pitch change. The firmware must mathematically cancel this coupling in real-time. When a pure roll is commanded, both motors are driven at identical speeds to prevent the bevel gear from walking.

Combat Orbit FSM

The Dynamic Sparring engine transforms the system from a coordinate-following actuator into a reactive sparring partner:

  1. Vector Alignment Skip: Skips windup when consecutive strikes share similar trajectories (threshold: 30°)
  2. Perimeter Transit Routing: Routes arm around workspace perimeter to avoid crossing the user's body zone
  3. Proportional Snap-Back: Dynamic recovery position maintains guard without returning to zero

Multi-Layered Safety

Layer Mechanism Response
Hardware Damiao built-in current limiter < 1 ms
Firmware Software dI/dt monitoring (Teensy 4.0) 5 ms
Application Kinematic auto-retract (GUI V3) ~50 ms

Low-Level Control

The arm actuation uses a 24V motor bus with CAN bus parallel connection (4 motors, 1 Mbps, 120 Ω termination) for motor communication, and an isolated 12V logic rail (dual-supply architecture) to prevent regenerative braking OVP trips from disrupting the Teensy/Jetson. The Teensy 4.0 runs a unified 200 Hz control loop integrating CAN motor commands, I²C sensor reads, and micro-ROS publishing.

Design Evolution Summary

The following table summarises the five major design iterations and the engineering rationale for each transition — spanning mechanical hardware, motor selection, structural revision, and motion planning logic:

Iteration Configuration Outcome Transition Rationale
1. Interim Gear-rail coaxial joint + servo motors DOF validated; servos failed under impact → BLDC motors required for impact resilience
2. ODrive ODrive V3.6 + 360KV outrunner FOC validated; jitter, no absolute position, too bulky → Integrated actuator required
3. Damiao DM-J4310-2EC + helical-spur 3:1 gearbox All requirements met; fair demo successful → Structural failures exposed at fair
4. Post-Fair Stainless D-shaft + Delrin pin hybrid interface Structural integrity validated; sustained sparring achieved → Motion quality insufficient (jerky return-to-zero)
5. Combat Orbit Stateless → State-Aware FSM (Combat Orbit) Fluid, continuous sparring; reduced mechanical wear from unnecessary return-to-zero cycles Current operational configuration: Euclidean skip-logic + proportional snap-back maintain kinetic fluidity

The structural failures and post-fair material science revisions are documented in Mechanical Design. The transition from stateless motion to the Combat Orbit FSM — driven by both mechanical longevity and training fidelity requirements — is documented in Firmware & Software.

Summary of Key Contributions

  1. Coaxial Differential Kinematics with Mass Centralisation: Closed-form forward and inverse kinematic equations were derived for the coupled 2-DOF joint, incorporating real-time parasitic pitch compensation. Motor mass centralisation at the pivot reduces moment of inertia, enabling human-competitive strike velocities.
  2. Combat Orbit FSM: A continuous-state sparring engine replaced stateless point-to-point motion, integrating vector alignment skip logic, perimeter transit routing, and proportional snap-back to produce fluid, uninterrupted combat sequences.
  3. Multi-Layered Safety Architecture: Three independent protection tiers — hardware current limiting, firmware dI/dt obstruction detection, and application-level kinematic auto-retract — provide defence-in-depth for both the drivetrain and the user.
  4. IMU-Based Strike Analytics: A Quad-IMU data acquisition pipeline — initially validated on an isolated rig at 500 Hz — was merged into the unified firmware for real-time punch detection via L2 acceleration vector norm thresholding.
  5. Regenerative Braking Mitigation & Dual-Rail Isolation: Software ramp-down and power rail separation resolved PSU OVP trips during high-speed sparring, ensuring uninterrupted system operation.
  6. Systematic Defect Resolution: Nine cross-layer integration defects — spanning CAN protocol parsing, I2C bus reliability, micro-ROS transport recovery, and regenerative braking — were diagnosed and resolved through structured root-cause analysis.
