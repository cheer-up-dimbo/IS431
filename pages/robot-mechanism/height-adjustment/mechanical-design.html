Mechanical Design

This page details the physical construction of the custom telescopic lift column — the separation of structural load paths, the materials selected, the Delrin bearing interfaces, and the gusseted joints where aluminium extrusion meets welded steel structure.

1. Decoupled Load Paths: Lift vs Structure

The defining design principle of BoxBunny's height-adjustment subsystem is the separation of axial lifting from lateral structural support. During operation, it experiences two fundamentally different types of loading:

  1. Axial (vertical) load: The predictable, steady downward weight of everything above the column (torso frame, arms, padding, sensors).
  2. Lateral loads and overturning moments: Dynamic, impulsive horizontal forces transmitted from user punches.

If a single mechanism is required to handle both simultaneously, the design becomes significantly more complex and prone to failure. The key decision was to treat the screw jack as purely an axial lifting device, while all lateral forces are resisted by the surrounding telescopic column structure.

This separation prevents side-loading of the screw jack, which would otherwise reduce efficiency, accelerate wear, and create a high risk of the nut binding on the screw under lateral punch impact.

2. Structural Layout & Fabrication

The final concept behaves less like an exposed actuator and more like a guided structural mast. The telescopic column comprises four primary elements:

Component Material Function
Moving inner member 8080 aluminium extrusion Carries the upper body; slides vertically within the outer tube.
Guide surfaces Delrin (acetal) wear pads Low-friction sacrificial interface between inner and outer members.
Fixed outer sleeve Welded steel tube Provides structural envelope; resists lateral loads and overturning moments.
Axial lifting device HK2T screw jack Carries axial load; provides height adjustment; self-locking.

SHS Wall Thickness (≥ 6 mm)

Large SHS members in the height-adjustment column are specified with a minimum wall thickness of 6 mm. Thicker walls increase the second moment of area (reducing deflection under punch impacts), ensure full-penetration welds at plate-to-tube intersections, and provide a flat, distortion-free internal bearing surface for the Delrin wear pads.

3. Delrin Bearings & Gusseted Joints

Delrin Bearing Interfaces

Delrin (acetal) wear pads are used as the bearing interface between the 8080 aluminium inner member and the steel outer tube. Delrin was selected because it provides a low friction coefficient, is self-lubricating, and offers sacrificial wear (so wear concentrates on the replaceable pads rather than the structural metals). The wear pads are positioned at the top and bottom of the outer tube, providing a wide moment arm for resisting tilting of the inner member.

Gusseted Aluminium-to-Steel Joints

Where the 8080 aluminium extrusion meets the welded steel structure, gusseted corner brackets are used to create robust, stiff connections. Because dissimilar metals cannot be easily welded, these bolted gussets provide a reliable joint that bridges across potential weld-distortion zones and distributes the large bending moments from punches over a wider area.

Fabrication Notes

A light-shock service factor of approximately 1.5 was adopted to account for minor bumps and start-stop transients during height adjustment. Internal clearances between inner and outer members are tightly controlled to prevent jamming while maintaining acceptable play for smooth operation.

4. Final Design State

The transition from the early dual-linear-guide concept to the final custom telescopic column eliminated the tolerance sensitivity and parallelism issues of exposed rails. The final design provides a clean aesthetic—the screw jack operates safely inside the column, hidden from view and protected from external disturbances.

Understanding this physical arrangement is critical because it dictates how forces flow through the system. When a punch lands on the elevated upper body, the resulting overturning moments and lateral forces must be transmitted down through this exact mechanical separation: routing lateral loads through the telescopic column and Delrin guides, while keeping the screw jack dedicated to axial lifting. The next section, Load Analysis, details how these structural loads and lifting forces were quantified to ensure the mechanism remains both safe and rigid under worst-case boxing conditions.
