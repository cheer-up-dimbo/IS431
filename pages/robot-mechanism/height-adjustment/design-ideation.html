Design Ideation

This page documents the three height-adjustment concepts evaluated for BoxBunny to satisfy system-level requirement RM-3 (Height Adjustability), the rejection rationale for each alternative, and the selection of the final manual screw jack with vertical linear guides architecture.

Design Constraints

The height adjustment system must vary the torso/head height by approximately 300–400 mm to accommodate different user heights, while:

  • Carrying the axial load of the upper structure (~20–25 kg including arms and padding)
  • Resisting lateral forces and overturning moments from punches
  • Remaining stiff and "non-bouncy" during training — once set, the height should not oscillate
  • Being safe and simple for users to adjust occasionally
  • Fitting within the available vertical envelope above the linear stage

Concept #1: Office Chair Mechanism + Vertical Linear Guide

The first concept adapted the office chair mechanism: a gas lift cylinder to carry the axial load, combined with vertical linear guides at the back to resist lateral punching forces.

Advantages

  • Gas lifts are readily available and compact
  • Can carry the static axial load of the robot mass
  • Vertical linear guides at the back can handle lateral loads and overturning moments

Rejection Rationale

  • Gas cylinders are inherently compliant. Under repeated punches, this leads to vertical "bouncing" of the torso, reducing strike rigidity.
  • The internal gas spring force varies with stroke; stiffness is not easily tuned.

Conclusion: Rejected — Insufficient vertical stiffness.

Concept #2: Electric Gas Strut + Motorised Vertical Linear Guide

The second concept combined an electrically actuated mechanism for height control with a gas strut at the bottom to assist in carrying axial load.

Advantages

  • Allows push-button height adjustment
  • The gas strut can reduce motor torque by counterbalancing part of the robot's weight

Rejection Rationale

  • Mechanically and electrically complex: coordinating motor drive, gas strut support, and guide loads is non-trivial.
  • Multiple elements share the load path, making stiffness and reliability harder to predict.

Conclusion: Rejected — Unnecessary complexity for an infrequent adjustment.

Concept #3: Screw Jack + Vertical Linear Guides (Selected)

The final concept uses a manual screw jack mounted on top of the linear stage, combined with vertical linear rails as primary guides.

  • The screw jack carries the axial load and provides precise height adjustment
  • Two vertical linear rails carry lateral forces and overturning moments from punches

Why This Concept Was Selected

  • High axial stiffness: screw jacks are designed for heavy-duty industrial applications.
  • Self-locking: non-backdrivable screw — height does not drift or bounce under impacts.
  • Simple load path: axial loads go directly through the screw jack; lateral loads go through the rails.
  • Robust and maintainable: standard industrial components with proven reliability.

Conclusion: Selected. Provides high holding stiffness, self-locking behaviour, and a clear separation of axial vs lateral support.
