Outboard Support & Packaging

Beyond the central slewing bearing and timing-belt drive, the rotation subsystem includes outboard support elements and integrated transport features. This page documents the cam-follower support system, the low-profile welded frame, and the rear transport wheels.

Cam-Follower Edge Supports

The central slewing bearing (010.10.120) carries the primary axial, radial, and moment loads. However, under off-axis punching — particularly hooks and angled straights — the upper structure experiences significant rocking (tilting) moments. Rather than forcing the central bearing alone to resist all of these, the system shares this task with discrete perimeter cam-follower roller assemblies.

Cam followers are stud-type roller bearings designed for track and cam applications. In BoxBunny, they are mounted at equally spaced positions around the outer edge of the rotating platform, rolling against a machined or welded track on the fixed base structure.

Function

  • Tilt stabilisation: The cam followers prevent the platform from tilting under off-axis loads. When a punch creates a rocking moment, the opposite cam follower engages its track and reacts the tilt force.
  • Increased effective support radius: By placing the cam followers at a larger radius than the slewing bearing, the effective moment arm for tilt resistance is increased, reducing the reaction forces on each individual element.
  • Load sharing: Static and dynamic loads are distributed between the central bearing and the perimeter supports, extending bearing life and reducing deflection.

Design Advantage

This approach avoids the need for a larger (and heavier, more expensive) central bearing to handle rocking moments alone. The modular nature of cam followers also allows the support system to be tuned — additional followers can be added or repositioned if testing reveals unexpected tilt behaviour.

Low-Profile Welded Frame

The rotation subsystem is packaged within a low-profile welded steel frame that sits inside the trapezoidal base structure. This frame serves multiple functions:

  • Bearing mount: The central slewing ring is bolted to a machined flat on the frame, ensuring proper alignment and load transfer to the base.
  • Motor mount: The BLDC servo motor is mounted to the fixed frame, with the driving pulley positioned at the correct centre-to-centre distance for belt tension.
  • Cam-follower track: The frame provides the bearing surface or track against which the perimeter cam followers roll.
  • Belt routing: The frame geometry accounts for belt path, idler/tensioner mounting, and access for belt installation and adjustment.

The "low-profile" requirement arises from the need to keep the rotation stage as close to ground level as possible. This minimises the overall robot height, keeps the slewing bearing near the ground (where it can best resist overturning moments), and preserves vertical space for the height-adjustment column above.

Transport Wheels

The rotation subsystem integrates the robot's rear transport wheels into its structural frame:

Component Specification Function
Transport wheels RMNA100, 100 mm diameter Provide smooth rolling on gym and corridor surfaces when robot is tilted for transport
Wheel pins KCLSBF12-62 Clevis-pin format for secure axle retention and easy wheel replacement

The wheels are mounted at the rear of the rotation frame, aligned with the wider rear section of the trapezoidal base. This placement ensures that the wheels are accessible for transport without interfering with the rotating platform, belt drive, or cam-follower supports.

During normal operation, the base rests flat on the floor and the wheels are either slightly elevated or at the same level as the base feet. When transport is needed, the operator tilts the entire robot rearward onto the wheels and rolls it to the new location — the same tip-and-roll strategy described in the Portability & Deployment page.

Integration with Base

The rotation frame is bolted into the trapezoidal base at defined mounting points. The integration was designed so that:

  • The rotation stage sits at the bottom of the robot stack — directly above the base and below the linear stage. This ensures the heaviest rotary support remains closest to the ground, where it can best resist overturning moments.
  • All fasteners are accessible from above or from the sides, allowing the rotation module to be serviced without full robot disassembly.
  • The transport wheels do not interfere with the working footprint when the robot is in its operational stance.
