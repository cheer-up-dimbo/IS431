Motion-Axis Selection

This page documents the foundational architecture decision for BoxBunny's lower-body motion, driven by system-level requirements RM-3 (Yaw Rotation) and RM-4 (Linear Translation): whether to use a single omnidirectional-wheel system or decoupled axes (separate yaw rotation and linear translation). The outcome directly establishes the need for a dedicated rotation subsystem.

Footwork Decomposition

From padwork observations and self-experiments, a coach's footwork can be decomposed into two essential motions:

  1. Linear translation (approach and retreat) along a primary training axis — corresponding to the coach stepping in/out as the boxer advances or retreats.
  2. Yaw rotation (on-the-spot turning) around a vertical axis — corresponding to the coach pivoting or re-angling to present new attack/defence lines.

The question is whether both motions should share a common hardware platform or be handled independently.

Omnidirectional Wheels

The omnidirectional wheel concept provides full planar mobility using a common set of wheels that enables X–Y translation and yaw rotation by appropriately commanding individual wheel speeds.

Benefits for Boxing Application

  • Single hardware stack handles all planar motions
  • Compact actuation with no additional bearings or sliding stages
  • Supports high-level commands like "move forward" or "rotate on the spot"
  • Well-established in general mobile robotics

Limitations for Punch-Receiving Robot

For a boxing trainer that must repeatedly absorb impulsive loads while maintaining stable stance, several critical limitations arise:

  • Floor dependence: Performance is highly sensitive to floor surface. Boxing gyms commonly use interlocking foam mats, rubber tiles, or cushioned flooring where wheels can sink, slip, or create unstable rolling conditions.
  • Pose creep: Small shifts accumulate over rounds under repeated impacts, making it difficult to maintain consistent stance reference relative to the user.
  • Low positional stiffness: Wheels provide limited resistance to position change under external forces — a punch can displace the robot from its commanded position before the control loop can correct.
  • Coupled motions: Disturbances on one axis affect all others, making precise yaw angle control under impact difficult.

Decoupled-Axis Architecture

A design that explicitly decouples the primary motions — using a linear rail for forward–backward range combined with a slewing bearing for yaw rotation — sacrifices omnidirectional mobility but introduces significant advantages for a punch-receiving application:

  • Floor-independent positioning: Both axes use mechanical guides (rails and bearings) rather than floor traction, eliminating sensitivity to surface conditions.
  • High positional stiffness: Preloaded linear blocks and a slewing ring bearing provide inherent resistance to external disturbances.
  • Decoupled control: Each axis can be controlled independently — a punch along one axis does not affect the other, simplifying the control architecture.
  • Repeatability: Mechanical guides provide consistent, repeatable positioning regardless of surface wear, temperature, or environmental conditions.

Decision Matrices

Formal decision matrices were constructed for both the linear and rotational axes, using a 1–5 rating scale (5 = best performance for BoxBunny's needs). High-priority criteria were weighted more heavily: stiffness, repeatability, and impact robustness were prioritised over cost and simplicity.

Forward-Backward Range Motion

Omnidirectional wheels score better on cost/simplicity as a single system, but the decoupled linear rail is significantly stronger on stiffness, repeatability, and floor independence.

Yaw Rotation (On-Spot Turning)

Omnidirectional wheels can rotate on the spot, but yaw stiffness and angle repeatability are limited by wheel slip and floor compliance. A slewing bearing driven by a motorised system offers high rotational stiffness and well-defined backlash, all independent of floor conditions.

Selection Outcome

Across both motions, the selection matrices confirm that omnidirectional wheels are attractive for general mobile robots but are penalised here by low stiffness, pose creep, and strong dependence on floor conditions. Decoupled axes (linear rail + slewing bearing) provide the stiff, repeatable, floor-independent geometry needed for a boxing trainer that must reliably hold stance under repeated punches.

BoxBunny therefore adopts a decoupled-axis architecture:

  • Motorised linear rail for forward–backward range
  • Slewing bearing with Motor drive for yaw rotation

This decision establishes the need for a dedicated rotation subsystem, documented further in the Bearing Selection and Drive Architecture pages.
